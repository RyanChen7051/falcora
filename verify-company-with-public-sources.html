How to verify a company with public sources

Trade show exhibitor records, association member directories and public tender notices are the three independent sources most teams can actually check. Here is what each one proves, what it does not prove, how strong its source link is, and why a 200 response is not the same as a live business.

Why one source is never enough

A company website is written by the company. A directory is written by a third party, but it may be out of date, and it may only prove attendance at one event. A tender notice proves a buyer-side appearance in a procurement process, not that the organisation wants to buy from you. Verification is the act of putting these separate, limited signals next to each other without letting any one of them fill a gap left by another.

Two levels of source strength

Strong: the link opens a page where the value you recorded is visible — typically a company's own contact or about page. Directory-level: the link opens an exhibition or association directory home page, and a reviewer must search the company name again. Both are legitimate citations, but they are not equivalent. Label which one you used.

1. The company's own official site

Best for basic company information and publicly listed role-based contacts. It is the only source that should be used for corporate contact details, and even then only for role addresses on the company's own domain. Personal contact details belong to a separate user-supplied layer and are never collected, inferred or displayed by the platform.

Record the exact URL that opened, including the protocol. Do not construct https:// by assumption. Follow redirects and note the landing page: a real domain may no longer be in the hands of the brand you expect.

2. Trade show exhibitor records

An exhibitor record can support one narrow claim: this company appeared in the exhibitor list of a named exhibition in a given year. It does not prove current trading volume, purchasing intent, or that the entity is a buyer. Exhibitor lists are supplier-side. Most expositions do not publish a stable per-company URL, so the honest citation is often the directory home page — a weaker source that requires a second search.

3. Association member directories

A public membership listing can support the claim that an organisation was listed as a member of a named industry association. Same caveats: supplier-side, possibly dated, and the citation frequently points at the directory rather than the row. An exhibitor record and a membership record are two different signals from two different publishers; neither substitutes for the other.

4. Public tender notices

Tender and procurement notices are the clearest buyer-side public signal of the three: the organisation appears in a notice as a purchaser. Cite the notice reference, the source URL and the retrieval date. A tender appearance still does not tell you who to call or whether a current requirement exists, and it should never be merged with supplier-side directory records into a single claim.

A reachability test, and why it changed our wording

In November 2026 we tested the official sites attached to 1,506 government-buyer records (1,220 unique domains). The observed states were:

841 reachableThe page opened and could be read.
168 blocked403 or 429: the site answered, but refused automated access.
2 hijackedA local filtering page impersonated the response.
495 no responseNothing came back. Not declared dead.

The last number is the important one. No response is not evidence that a business has closed; it can simply mean the site blocks automated requests. We record the state as observed, keep the exact URL that worked in a separate field, and never promote a non-response into a dead-company conclusion. The same discipline applies to 403 and 429 responses, which are still collected and labelled as blocked rather than discarded.

The mistake that invalidates a report

Using a supplier-side exhibitor listing as evidence that a company is a buyer, using one directory to fill a blank left by another, or turning an empty field into a plausible value. Any one of these makes a report look more complete while making it less true.

Common questions

What public sources can verify that a company is real?

Use several unrelated public sources: the company's own official site for basics and role-based contacts, trade show exhibitor records, association member directories, and public tender notices. Each answers a different question. Keep them separate in your file and attach a source link and retrieval date to every line.

Is a trade show exhibitor list proof that a company is a buyer?

No. Exhibitor lists and association member directories are supplier-side records: they show that a company exhibited or held a membership. Public tender notices are buyer-side evidence. Tag each record with its side. Combining suppliers and buyers into one claim is a common way to produce a convincing but wrong report.

Why does a directory source link open a home page instead of the company row?

Many exhibition and association directories do not expose a stable per-company URL, so the honest citation is the directory home page. In a report this is a weaker source than a company website link, where the value is visible on the page you open. Mark the difference: a directory citation means a reviewer must search the company name again.

Does HTTP 200 mean the company website is reachable?

No. A 200 response only means something answered. A 403 or 429 usually indicates anti-bot protection rather than a closed business, a local filtering page can impersonate a response, and a domain may no longer be controlled by the brand. Record the exact state you saw and the exact URL that opened; never assume a protocol or declare a site dead from a non-response.

How should I cite a source in a due diligence report?

Give the exact source URL, the value as it appears there, and the retrieval date, then assign one of three states: found, found but empty, or not found. If the link points to a directory home page, say so. Never replace a missing value with one borrowed from another source, and never add a sample value.
